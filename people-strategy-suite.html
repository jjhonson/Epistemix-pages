The Challenge

The responsibility has grown. The tools haven't kept up.

Chief People Officers are expected to manage workforce cost, drive organizational performance, compete for talent in a market that doesn't stand still, and build an employer brand that attracts the people the company's strategy requires.

Most of the tools available were built for a different job. They analyze what your employee population has done historically. They don't tell you what it is likely to do next, or how different decisions would change that outcome. When consequential decisions are made with backward-looking analysis or external consultants who deliver a report and move on, you're managing the workforce you have rather than building the one you need.

Cost Pressures

Benefits costs, labor costs, and turnover costs compound year over year. The levers available to most CPOs are blunt and reactive rather than precise and forward-looking.

Performance Pressures

Productivity, engagement, and organizational health are hard to optimize when the data you're working with reflects what already happened.

Competitive Pressures

The talent market moves faster than network renewal cycles. Organizations winning the competition for people are making faster, better-informed decisions.

Employer Brand

The benefits and programs you offer signal what kind of company you are. The right investments compound over time. The wrong ones are expensive noise.

The Opportunity

The CPO who runs a data-driven function changes what the role means.

The most effective people leaders today don't think of their function as a cost center. They think of it as the mechanism through which the company acquires, develops, and retains the human capital its strategy requires.

That means making decisions about benefits, compensation, workforce composition, and workplace investment with the same analytical rigor a CFO brings to capital allocation. It means being able to explain every significant decision with data. It means being forward-looking about what the workforce will need to look like, not just reactive to what it looks like today.

What's Different

Forward-looking simulation built for the decisions you actually make.

We build a synthetic model of your specific workforce and simulate how different decisions would affect it under conditions that don't exist yet. Not what happened last year. What is likely to happen as your organization grows, relocates, and changes.

Model the workforce you're going to have

When you're evaluating a benefits network, we simulate how it would perform as you hire in new cities and your workforce shifts demographically, not just how it would have served your workforce last year.

Run scenarios before committing

Own the capability, not the report

Hold your advisors accountable

The Modules

Three decisions. Modeled forward.

Each module addresses a specific high-stakes decision in the people function. They share the same data foundation, so each one you add is faster to deploy and more accurate than the last.

Module 01

Network Selection

Choose the health plan networks that best serve your workforce today and as your organization grows. Model access, cost, and disruption across multiple configurations before committing.

Currently Replaced By

Benefits consultants running historical claims analysis. Static reports that reflect last year's workforce composition and geography.

What You Get

Forward-looking simulation of how different network configurations perform as you hire in new locations and your workforce evolves. Multiple scenarios with clear trade-offs across access, cost, and coverage.

Module 02

Point Solution Evaluation

Know which supplemental benefits will actually move the needle for your specific employee population before you sign a contract and allocate per-member budget.

Currently Replaced By

Vendor presentations, industry benchmarks, and intuition. Benefits decisions made on what sounds good rather than what your population is likely to respond to.

What You Get

ROI modeling for each point solution against your actual employee population. Projected adoption rates, health impact, and cost-effectiveness grounded in your data, not category averages.

Module 03

Risk Stratification

Identify the members of your workforce most likely to generate significant health costs in the next one to two years, while there is still time to intervene effectively.

Currently Replaced By

Reactive cost management after high-cost claims have already occurred. Stop loss coverage purchased without a clear view of where the risk actually sits in the population.

What You Get

Early identification of high-risk members with modeled intervention options and projected cost and health outcomes for each path. Act before costs materialize rather than after.
