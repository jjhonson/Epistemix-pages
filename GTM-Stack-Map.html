The rule behind Have / Now / Next / Wait

A tool earns Now only when three things are all true: the job it does is a bottleneck on a motion we're actually running today, its prerequisites already exist, and volume is high enough that the tool beats doing it by hand.

If the job optimizes a motion we haven't built yet, it's Wait (with a named trigger). If the job is real and near but its prerequisite isn't in place, it's Next (queued behind that prerequisite). If we could do it by hand at current volume, it waits.

The practical read: Now is a short list on purpose. Next is the on-deck queue once the enabling hire or motion lands (mostly the DoM/enrichment owner and the Content Lead). Wait is the optimization layer for a machine we're still building, and most of it is correctly empty right now. That emptiness is the point: it's the shape of the function we're growing into, not a backlog of gaps to close this quarter.
